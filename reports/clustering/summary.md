# Clustering Comparison: k-means vs EM (GMM) vs Fuzzy c-means — v0.1.0

Generated by `src/cluster_compare.py`. 86 distilleries, 12 flavour scores (0–4), z-scored.

## Method notes
- **k-means:** hard labels, 12-D, k=5 (original paper's elbow choice).
- **EM / Gaussian mixture:** fit on the first 3 principal components (53% of variance) with a tied covariance.
  A full 12-D fit on 86 rows overfits: BIC kept falling and every membership came out at 1.00.
- **Fuzzy c-means:** fuzzifier m=1.2. The textbook m=2 collapses in 12-D, giving all 86 distilleries a uniform 0.20 membership.

## Findings
| Method | Silhouette (k=5) | ARI vs k-means | ARI vs region | Ambiguous (<0.6) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| k-means | 0.149 | 1.00 | 0.112 | n/a (hard) |
| EM / GMM | 0.105 | 0.61 | 0.087 | 5 |
| Fuzzy c-means | 0.118 | 0.65 | 0.079 | 12 |

1. **BIC selects k=2, not 5.** EM's likelihood-based model selection supports two groups: a
   6-distillery peated/medicinal group (Ardbeg, Caol Ila, Lagavulin, Laphroaig, Talisker, Clynelish)
   and everything else. Silhouette agrees (0.31 at k=2 vs 0.15 at k=5).
2. **The 5-cluster solution is weak.** Silhouettes of ≤0.15 mean that, beyond peat, flavour is a continuum rather than distinct groups.
3. **Clusters barely track region** (ARI ≈ 0.1). Region alone is a poor proxy for flavour, except Islay (4 of 7 in the peated group).
4. **Soft membership flags bridge distilleries** (e.g., Craigellachie, Glengarioch, Glenrothes) that sit between profiles.
   These are candidates for crossover marketing and blending, which a hard k-means label hides.
